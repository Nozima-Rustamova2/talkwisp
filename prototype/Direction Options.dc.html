Turn 1 · visual direction

Two directions, same two screens

Identical content in both, so the only variable is the visual language. Left column is the momentum register (sign-in), right is the ledger register (review queue) — the screen that has to survive whatever we pick. The review-queue content here is a visual probe, not the screen D design; I haven't described D yet and I'm not designing it ahead.

1A Industry, modernised same system, four token changes

8px radius and a soft shadow instead of a hairline border; accent pushed from #5980a6 to a saturated #3d78b8; white input fills with real internal padding; body copy moved off Barlow onto a neutral system sans, condensed headings kept. Registration marks survive on the primary button only — with cards unframed there is nothing left to register.

1B Telegram-fluent utility a different direction

The interface idiom this owner already reads fluently every day is Telegram: white surfaces on a cool ground, one confident blue, rounded soft-shadowed cards instead of frames, generous touch targets, no chrome that isn't load-bearing. Not Telegram's brand — its plainness. One variable font (Manrope, drawn with Cyrillic) at two weights, so it's a single file on a slow connection and Cyrillic and Latin sit at the same optical weight in one row. Hierarchy comes from elevation and one accent, not from framing everything equally.
